## Your Best XI Alone Won't Win

Football Manager 2026 punishes managers who refuse to rotate. Multi-competition seasons, the new match engine's physical demands, and the morale system all conspire against you if you run the same eleven every match.

Rotation isn't just about rest — it's about keeping your entire squad motivated, match-fit, and ready when called upon.

---

## Part 1: Why Rotation Is Non-Negotiable

| Benefit | Why It Matters |
|---------|---------------|
| **Injury prevention** | High fatigue → increased injury risk → lost key players |
| **Squad morale** | Bench players need game time or they become unhappy |
| **Match fitness** | Substitutes lose sharpness without regular minutes |
| **Form dips** | Every player hits bad form — you need alternatives ready |
| **Promise fulfillment** | Breaking playing-time promises destroys dressing room trust |

In FM26, even condition icons showing green can be deceiving. The true fatigue number (hidden behind the icon) accumulates over multiple matches and only appears when it's critical. **Always check the Medical Centre**, not just the condition icon.

---

## Part 2: Reading the Medical Centre

Navigate to **Medical → Overview** for your most important weekly check:

| Metric | What It Tells You | Action |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| **Overall Injury Risk** | Composite score of fatigue + match load | Red/orange = rest several players |
| **Match Load** | Matches played + average distance covered in last 14 days | Heavy load = plan a rest game |
| **Training Load** | Additional strain from sessions | High + high match load = injury bomb |
| **Injury Susceptibility** | Combined history + current load | High-risk players need extra rotation |
| **Fatigue** | Deep fatigue beyond condition icon | Rest even if condition shows green |
| **Match Sharpness** | How ready a player is to perform | Below 80% sharpness = risk poor performance |

### The 14-Day Rule
Look at match load over a rolling 14-day window. Any player with **3+ starts in 14 days** in an intensive tactic (high press, high tempo) needs a rest game or reduced minutes.

## Part 3: The Squad Planner — Your Rotation Compass

FM26's Squad Planner is the single best tool for managing rotation. Use it proactively:

### Setup
1. Map every position with **2 players**
2. Mark your **definitive starters** vs **rotation options**
3. Identify **versatile players** who can cover multiple positions — they're worth their weight in gold

### The Versatility Premium
Players who can cover 3+ positions comfortably are rotation heroes:
- Full-back who plays both sides
- CB who can also play DM
- Winger who can play as a striker in a pinch
- These players give you tactical flexibility when rotating

### Role Compatibility Check
FM26's dual-role system means your rotation player must be competent in **both** the In-Possession and Out-of-Possession roles you've assigned. Don't throw a poor-tackling midfielder into a Ball-Winning Midfielder role just because they're available.

## Part 4: The Rotation Framework

### Weekly Two-Match Schedule
| Match | Lineup | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Midweek (Cup / Europe)** | Rotated XI (~7 changes) | Rest starters, keep rotation players sharp |
| **Weekend (League)** | Full-strength XI | Maximize league points |

### Three-Match in 7 Days (Crisis Week)
- Match 1 (Saturday): Full-strength XI
- Match 2 (Tuesday/Wednesday): Rotate 5-7 players
- Match 3 (Saturday): Best available given fatigue data
- Training: Minimal — Recovery + Match Preview only

### Rotation by Position Priority
Some positions demand more rotation than others:

| Position | Rotation Frequency | Why |
|----------|-------------------|-----|
| Wing-Backs (WB/FB on Attack) | Every other game | Most physically demanding role — constant sprints |
| Box-to-Box Midfielder | Every other game | Highest distance covered in most systems |
| Pressing Forward | Rest every 3rd game | Constant pressing drains stamina fast |
| Centre-Backs | Rest every 5-6 games | Least physically demanding outfield role |
| Goalkeeper | Almost never | Condition barely decreases |

### Youth Integration as Rotation
Cup matches against lower-league opposition, dead-rubber group games, and the last 15 minutes of comfortable wins are where you introduce youth prospects. They get match experience; your starters get rest.

## Part 5: Morale — The Hidden Performance Multiplier

Player morale in FM26 affects every attribute — a happy player with 15 Finishing outperforms an unhappy one with 17. Morale management is as important as tactical management.

### Morale Check Routine
Every Monday, check **Squad → Happiness**:
- Any player below "Good" morale? Investigate.
- Any player with an outstanding promise? Resolve it this week.

### Keeping the Bench Happy
The hardest morale challenge: keeping your second-choice players content.

| Playing Time Promise | Minimum Appearances |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Star Player** | Start 80%+ of matches when fit |
| **Important Player** | Start 60%+ of matches |
| **Regular Starter** | Start ~50%, regular sub appearances |
| **Squad Player** | Rotational — occasional starts, frequent subs |
| **Impact Sub** | Sub appearances — don't need starts |
| **Future Prospect** | Youth team or occasional bench |

> **Warning**: Never promise "Star Player" or "Important Player" to someone you view as a rotation option. Players track their playing time and will confront you if you break promises.

### Handling the "More Playing Time" Demand
- **Justified request** (player performing well, genuinely underused) → Promise more starts, then deliver
- **Unjustified request** (poor form, adequate playing time) → Refuse firmly, but monitor morale
- **Never** promise playing time you can't fulfill — broken promises are worse than refused requests

## Part 6: Common Rotation Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|------------|-----|
| Rotating 8+ players at once | Lost rhythm, disjointed performance | Rotate 4-7 players max per match |
| Benching a star player too often | Player unhappy, "Important Player" promise broken | Rotate star players only when fatigue demands it |
| Ignoring morale of rotation players | Unhappiness spreads through squad | Regular check-ins, occasional starts |
| Failing to fulfill contract promises | Dressing room crisis | Be honest in contract negotiations about playing time |
| Playing a player immediately after injury recovery | Re-injury risk spikes | 15 min → 30 min → 45 min → 60 min → start — gradual reintroduction |
| Playing unfit/low-sharpness players | Poor performance + injury risk | Check Medical Centre before every match day |

## Part 7: The Injury Recovery Protocol

When a player returns from injury:
1. **First match back**: 15-20 minutes as a sub
2. **Second match**: 30 minutes as a sub
3. **Third match**: 45 minutes (sub off at halftime)
4. **Fourth match**: 60 minutes (sub off)
5. **Fifth match**: Ready for a full start

Skipping these steps dramatically increases re-injury risk. The Medical Centre will flag "elevated injury risk" on players returning too quickly.

## Seasonal Rotation Checklist

- [ ] Pre-season: Set up Squad Planner with 2 players per position
- [ ] Weekly: Check Medical Centre for fatigue and injury risk
- [ ] Monday: Review Squad Happiness — address any morale issues
- [ ] Pre-match: Check match sharpness of planned starters (80%+ required)
- [ ] Post-match: Rotate based on fatigue data for next fixture
- [ ] Monthly: Review playing time promises — any at risk?
- [ ] Injury returns: Gradual reintroduction protocol
- [ ] Youth cup appearances: Scheduled rotation for prospect development
