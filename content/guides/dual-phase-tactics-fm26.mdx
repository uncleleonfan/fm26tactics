## The Biggest Tactical Feature of FM26

Dual-phase tactics let you set **two different formations**: one for when your team has the ball (in possession) and one for when you don't (out of possession). Instead of the old system where one shape was stretched and pulled out of place, your team now *transitions* between two distinct structures.

This single feature changes everything about how FM26 tactics are built — and most guides on the internet still haven't caught up. Here's how to master it.

---

## Why Two Shapes Beats One

In FM24-style football, a 4-3-3 with a high press defended with the same 4-3-3 — which meant gaps in wide areas and a lonely striker. With dual-phase tactics you can:

- **Attack with three at the back, defend with four.** The wing-back drops in during the defensive phase.
- **Defend with a compact 4-4-2, attack with a fluid 3-2-5.** The wide midfielders push up and one full-back inverts.
- **Press high with 9 players, but retreat into a mid-block 4-1-4-1** the moment the ball goes past you.

The engine favours this flexibility. A team that can reshape between phases wins the midfield battle more consistently — you get the best of both worlds instead of compromising on one.

---

## How Dual-Phase Actually Works in FM26

In the **Tactics** screen, the In Possession / Out of Possession tabs now each carry their own formation selector:

1. **In Possession (IP) shape** — the formation your team lines up in when building up and attacking.
2. **Out of Possession (OOP) shape** — the formation your team drops into when defending and pressing.

The game interpolates between the two during transitions, so a radical shift like 3-4-3 → 4-4-2 happens smoothly as the ball turns over.

### Setting It Up

- Open **Tactics → In Possession** and set your attacking shape.
- Open **Tactics → Out of Possession** and set your defensive shape.
- Assign roles in both phases. Some roles behave differently depending on the phase — this is where the new FM26 roles shine.

> **Pro tip:** start with one shape and only add the second phase once your team is fluid with the first. Radical dual-phase setups (e.g. 3-2-5 attack → 5-4-1 defend) take longer for players to learn.

## The New FM26 Roles That Make Dual-Phase Sing

Dual-phase tactics were designed alongside a batch of new roles. These four are the ones that let you change shape without changing personnel:

### Channel Midfielder (CM)

A midfielder who attacks the **half-spaces** between the lines. In a 4-3-3 → 4-4-2 transition, the Channel Midfielder becomes the second striker in the defensive phase — pressing from the front without needing a substitution. He's the connective tissue between midfield and attack.

- **Key attributes:** Off the Ball, Acceleration, Passing, First Touch, Decisions
- **Best pairings:** 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 3-4-3 — see the [Channel Midfielder role guide](/roles/channel-midfielder)

### Playmaking Wing-Back (PWB)

The wing-back who **drifts inside** to dictate play, creating overloads in central areas while the wide forward stretches the pitch. In dual-phase, the PWB can push up in possession (3-4-3 → 3-2-5) and drop into a flat back four out of possession.

- **Key attributes:** Passing, Crossing, Vision, Dribbling, Work Rate
- **Best pairings:** 3-5-2, 3-4-3, 5-2-3 — see the [Playmaking Wing-Back role guide](/roles/playmaking-wing-back)

### Overlapping Centre-Back (OCB)

The third-centre-back who **steps out and overlaps** on the flank in possession. He's the reason a 3-4-3 can attack like a 2-4-4 — and why it can still defend like a back five. The OCB is *the* role that makes "attack with three, defend with five" actually work.

- **Key attributes:** Pace, Off the Ball, Passing, Work Rate
- **Best pairings:** 3-5-2, 5-3-2, 3-4-3 — see the [Overlapping Centre-Back role guide](/roles/overlapping-centre-back)

### Line-Holding Keeper (LHK)

The traditional goalkeeper who **holds his line** instead of sweeping. In a dual-phase system with a low defensive block, the LHK stays deep behind a compact back line — while a sweeper keeper works better if your defensive phase presses high. Choose based on your OOP shape, not your IP shape.

- **Key attributes:** Aerial Reach, Reflexes, Handling, Positioning
- **Best pairings:** 4-4-2, 5-3-2, 4-2-3-1 — see the [Line-Holding Keeper role guide](/roles/line-holding-keeper)

## Three Proven Dual-Phase Blueprints

### 1. The Guardiola: 3-2-5 in Possession → 4-3-3 Out of Possession

The classic positional-play shape-shift, now native in FM26.

| Phase | Shape | Key roles |
|---|---|---|
| In possession | 3-2-5 | OCB, Playmaking Wing-Back (inverts), Channel Midfielder |
| Out of possession | 4-3-3 | OCB drops to full-back, PWB drops into midfield |

**Why it works:** you get a winger-heavy 3-2-5 in attack (five players in the final third) and a solid 4-3-3 out of possession. The OCB and PWB are the two players who switch roles between phases — that's the entire trick.

### 2. The Catenaccio: 3-5-2 → 5-4-1

A deep defensive block that still creates with three at the back.

| Phase | Shape | Key roles |
|---|---|---|
| In possession | 3-5-2 | Wing-backs high, Target Forward up top |
| Out of possession | 5-4-1 | Wing-backs drop, CM becomes a shuttling fourth midfielder |

**Why it works:** the Line-Holding Keeper sits behind a five-man wall, and the wing-backs become wide defenders when the ball is lost. Perfect for underdog saves — see our [3-5-2 Catenaccio breakdown](/tactics/3-5-2-catenaccio).

### 3. The Mid-Block: 4-2-3-1 → 4-4-2

A balanced option that protects the centre without sacrificing width.

| Phase | Shape | Key roles |
|---|---|---|
| In possession | 4-2-3-1 | Advanced Playmaker in the hole |
| Out of possession | 4-4-2 | The AM and the striker form a front two |

**Why it works:** pressing in a 4-4-2 block is far easier to coach than a 4-2-3-1 press — the front two cut passing lanes together. This is the most beginner-friendly dual-phase setup. Start here before attempting Blueprint 1. See our [4-2-3-1 Gegenpress](/tactics/4-2-3-1-gegenpress) and the [4-4-2 Wing Play](/tactics/4-4-2-wing-play) breakdowns.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Two shapes too similar.** If your IP and OOP shapes are basically the same, you're wasting the feature. Force at least one positional change between phases.
2. **Ignoring transition fitness.** Switching between shapes every attack is demanding. Your wing-backs and CMs need high **Work Rate and Stamina** or the system collapses after 60 minutes.
3. **Forgetting the keeper.** Your goalkeeper's role should match your OOP shape — LHK for deep blocks, Sweeper Keeper for high pressing defensive phases.
4. **Learning time.** A radical dual-phase setup needs more pre-season training time. If you're switching tactics mid-season, expect 6–10 matches of adjustment.

Dual-phase tactics are the single biggest skill gap in the FM26 community right now. Most players are still using single-shape tactics built for older versions — which means anyone who masters the two-shape system has a genuine in-game and content advantage.
