## Why Build Your Own Tactic?

Downloaded tactics can win you trophies, but building your own tactic is where FM26 truly shines. When you craft a system from scratch, you understand every decision — and when things go wrong (they will), you know exactly what to adjust.

This guide provides a structured framework for building any tactic from the ground up.

## Phase 1: Define Your Philosophy (15 Minutes)

Before opening the tactics screen, answer these four questions:

### 1. What's your identity?
- Are you an attacking team or a defensive one?
- Do you want possession or direct football?
- Do you press high or sit back?

**Write down ONE sentence**: "We are a [style] team that [key behavior]."

Example: "We are a high-pressing team that wins the ball in the opposition's half."

### 2. What's your squad profile?
Check your squad comparison screen (Squad → Analyst Report → Comparison):

| Attribute | If High | If Low |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Work Rate | Press high | Sit back |
| Pace | Counter-attack | Possession |
| Passing | Short passing | Direct passing |
| Jumping Reach | Cross often | Pass through middle |

### 3. What's your club context?
- **Top team**: You'll face parked buses → need possession and creativity
- **Mid-table**: Mix of games → need tactical flexibility
- **Underdog**: You'll be defending → need counter-attack and set pieces

### 4. What's your non-negotiable?
One thing your tactic MUST do. Examples:
- "We must press from the front"
- "Full-backs must overlap"
- "At least 60% possession"

## Phase 2: Choose Your Formation (10 Minutes)

Match your formation to your philosophy:

| Philosophy | Best Formations | Why |
|-----------|----------------|-----|
| High press | 4-2-3-1, 4-3-3 | Natural pressing structure, forward numbers |
| Possession | 4-3-3, 3-4-3 | Midfield triangles, passing options |
| Counter | 4-4-2, 3-5-2 | Compact block, two strikers for breaks |
| Direct | 4-4-2, 4-4-1-1 | Clear passing structure, target man options |

**The midfield rule**: Count your central midfielders vs the most common opposition formation (usually 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3). You need at least a 3v3 centrally — never be outnumbered in midfield.

## Phase 3: Select Player Roles (20 Minutes)

This is where most homemade tactics succeed or fail. Follow these principles:

### The 5-Lane Rule
A balanced tactic should cover all five vertical lanes:

```
Left    Left       Right    Right
Wide   Half-Spc   Center   Half-Spc  Wide
  ↓       ↓         ↓         ↓        ↓
  FB    CM/IF      DM/CM    CM/IF     FB/W
```

If two players occupy the same lane with the same role, you create congestion and passing redundancies. Each lane should have one primary occupant.

### Role Combinations That Work

**Midfield trios**:
- DLP(D) + BBM(S) + AP(A) — classic creator-runner-destroyer
- DM(D) + CAR(S) + MEZ(A) — wide movement and half-space overloads
- CM(D) + DLP(S) + CM(A) — balanced with late runs

**Strike partnerships**:
- AF(A) + DLF(S) — runner + creator (most versatile)
- TF(A) + AF(A) — aerial outlet + speed (wing play)
- PF(A) + CF(S) — pressing + technical (gegenpress)

**Wing combinations**:
- W(S) + FB(A) — winger holds width, full-back underlaps
- IF(A) + FB(S) — inside forward attacks space, full-back balances
- IW(S) + WB(A) — inverted winger creates, wing-back provides width

### The Support-Attack-Defend Balance

As a rule of thumb for any formation:

| Formation | Defend | Support | Attack |
|-----------|--------|---------|--------|
| 4-4-2 | 3-4 | 4-5 | 2-3 |
| 4-3-3 | 3 | 4-5 | 3 |
| 4-2-3-1 | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| 3-5-2 | 3-4 | 4-5 | 3 |

Having 3 players on Defend duty ensures you're never caught short. Having 3 on Attack ensures attacking threat. The rest on Support provides flexibility.

## Phase 4: Set Team Instructions (15 Minutes)

### The Minimum Viable Tactic
Start with ONLY these instructions and add more after watching how they play:

**In Possession** (pick 2-3):
- Attacking width
- Passing directness
- Tempo

**In Transition** (pick 1-2):
- Counter or Counter-press
- Distribution method

**Out of Possession** (pick 3-4):
- Defensive line
- Line of engagement
- Pressing intensity
- Defensive width

### The Instruction Compatibility Matrix

Some instructions conflict. Use this quick reference:

| Combined | Result |
|----------|--------|
| Much Shorter Passing + Much Higher Tempo | Contradiction — short passes need time |
| Much Higher Defensive Line + Less Urgent Pressing | Contradiction — high line needs pressure on the ball |
| Counter + Hold Shape | Contradiction — can't counter and hold shape |
| Play Out of Defense + Distribute Quickly | Contradiction — buildup vs direct |

## Phase 5: Test and Refine (3-5 Matches)

### What to Watch (Comprehensive Highlights, First 15 Mins)

**Defensive issues**:
- [ ] Opposition getting behind your defensive line → Lower it or add Cover duty
- [ ] Crosses finding targets → Check full-back positioning, defensive width
- [ ] Through balls splitting center-backs → Check gap between CBs, add DM protection
- [ ] Set pieces conceded → Review set piece setup separately

**Attacking issues**:
- [ ] Shots from distance only → Add "Work Ball Into Box," check support options
- [ ] No shots at all → Check attacking width, passing risk, mentality
- [ ] Crossing to nobody → Check striker roles, add "Low Crosses" for small strikers
- [ ] Possession but no penetration → Increase tempo, add "Pass Into Space"

**Transition issues**:
- [ ] Counter-attacks always broken → Check passing directness, forward roles
- [ ] Conceding on counters → Check midfield duties, defensive line height
- [ ] Losing second balls → Check physical attributes, increase pressing triggers

### The 5-Match Rule
Give any tactical change at least 5 full matches before judging it. FM26 has a tactical familiarity system — new instructions take time to bed in.

## Phase 6: Build Your Plan B

Every tactic has a weakness. Build a second tactic that covers it:

| Main Tactic | Plan B Should |
|-------------|---------------|
| Gegenpress | Possession system (for when you're up 2-0 and need to rest) |
| Counter-attack | Attacking system (for when you need a goal) |
| Possession | Direct system (for bad weather, tight pitches) |
| Wing play | Central overload (for when crosses aren't working) |

Train your Plan B to full familiarity during pre-season so it's ready when needed.
