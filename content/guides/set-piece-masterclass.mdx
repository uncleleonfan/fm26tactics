## Why Set Pieces Win Titles

In Football Manager 2026, well-designed set piece routines can deliver **10-15 extra goals per season**. That's the difference between 4th and 1st in tight leagues. Yet most managers leave corners on default settings, leaving goals on the table.

This guide covers three battle-tested corner routines and the framework for designing your own.

---

## Part 1: The FM26 Corner Kick Meta

The community has converged on three dominant archetypes. Switch between them based on your personnel and the opponent:

| Routine | Best For | Risk Level |
|---------|----------|------------|
| **Near-Post Routine** | Teams with strong aerial threats | Low |
| **Far-Post Overload** | When near-post gets defended | Medium |
| **Short Corner Overload** | Teams lacking aerial dominance | Low-Medium |

---

## Part 2: The Near-Post Routine (The Meta Standard)

The most consistent routine in the current match engine. Simple, repeatable, devastating.

### Setup
1. **Corner Taker**: High Corners + Technique. Aim at **Near Post**.
2. **Near Post Attack (Primary Target)**: Your best header — a CB or tall striker. Max Jumping Reach + Heading + Bravery.
3. **Far Post Attack**: Secondary aerial threat. Attack Far Post.
4. **Near Post Flick On**: Player positioned to flick crosses onward at near post.
5. **Lurk Outside Area**: One midfielder with high Long Shots — collects clearances and recycle.
6. **Stay Back**: Two players (usually full-backs) to prevent counters.

### Delivery
- **Aim**: Near Post
- **Type**: In-swinging
- Place the cursor at the **near-post sweet spot** (approximately 6-yard line, 3-4 yards from goal)

### Why It Works
The near-post area is the hardest for goalkeepers to command. A well-placed in-swinger forces the keeper to stay on his line while your aerial threat attacks the ball. Even slight deflections often result in goals.

## Part 3: Far-Post Overload

The counter to teams that over-defend the near post.

### Setup
1. **Corner Taker**: High Corners + Crossing. Aim at **Far Post**.
2. **Far Post Attack**: TWO players attacking the far post. One primary target (big), one cleanup (intelligent).
3. **Near Post Attack**: One player — purely to drag defenders.
4. **Attack Ball From Edge**: A midfielder (Mezzala or BBM) who times a late run into the box.
5. **Lurk Outside Area**: Long-shot specialist.
6. **Stay Back**: Two players.

### Delivery
- **Aim**: Far Post
- **Type**: High arc or in-swinging
- Target the far-post sweet spot — the area between the penalty spot and the far post

### When to Switch
- Opponent has 3+ defenders on near post
- Your near-post routine has produced 0 chances in 3+ consecutive corners
- You have a pace mismatch at the far side

## Part 4: Short Corner Overload (The Sophisticated Option)

Best for technical teams that lack aerial dominance. This routine punishes defenses that commit numbers to the box by pulling them out of position.

### Setup
1. **Corner Taker**: High Technique + Composure.
2. **Short Corner Receiver** (Crucial): Your most creative winger or advanced full-back. Stationed 5-8 yards from the corner taker.
3. **Near Post Attack**: 2 players — decoys that occupy defenders.
4. **Far Post Attack**: 1 player.
5. **Edge of Area**: 2 players. One cuts in, one stays deeper for the secondary pass.
6. **Stay Back**: 2 players.

### The Sequence
1. Take short → Receiver draws a defender out
2. Receiver has two options:
   - **Option A**: Return pass to the corner taker who's now unmarked for a cross
   - **Option B**: Through-pass to the edge-of-area runner
3. If the cross comes in, the box is less crowded because defenders tracked the short option

### Key Attribute Requirements
- **Receiver**: Acceleration, Decisions, First Touch
- **Edge runners**: Long Shots, Composure, Off the Ball
- **Box attackers**: Anticipation, Off the Ball, Finishing

## Part 5: Set Piece Design Framework

No single routine works forever. Use this framework to adjust:

### Analyze → Adjust → Test cycle

1. **After 5 matches** → Check Data Hub → Set Pieces
   - Shots from corners / free kicks
   - Headers won in the box
   - Goals from set plays
2. **Adjust one variable** → Aim point, delivery type, or personnel
3. **Test 5 more matches** → Compare

### Universal Rules
- **Always train Set Piece routines** — uncoached routines underperform by 30%+
- **Rotate your takers** — fatigue reduces corner quality late in matches
- **Adjust per opponent** — check their Set Piece defending stats in Data Hub
- **Set defensive routines too** — "Stay Back" assignments are just as important as attacking ones

## Part 6: Free Kicks & Long Throws

### Direct Free Kicks
- Assign your best Free Kick taker + Long Shots player
- Train the "Tries Long Range Free Kicks" trait
- Aiming at the corner where the keeper is positioned is the most effective in FM26's match engine

### Indirect Free Kicks (Wide)
- Treat these like corners — use the same near/far post setup
- Add one extra player "Attack Ball" since the angle is more favorable

### Long Throws
- A player with 15+ Long Throws + "Possesses Long Flat Throw" trait is a weapon
- Position setup identical to corners
- Train Long Flat Throws as a trait for one full-back
