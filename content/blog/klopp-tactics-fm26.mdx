The gegenpress is the most misrepresented tactic in Football Manager. It gets described as "high pressing, high tempo, high line", which is true the way "a car has wheels" is true. It tells you nothing about why it works, and nothing about why yours stops working in the 65th minute.

Klopp's Liverpool were not organised around intensity. They were organised around three specific things: a trigger map that told each player exactly when to move, a six-second window after which the press was abandoned, and a rest defence that made the whole thing survivable when it failed.

Get those three right and the intensity takes care of itself. Get them wrong and you have a team of eleven players sprinting in different directions.

## The trigger map

The single biggest mistake with a gegenpress in FM26 is pressing everything. Eleven players chasing the ball is not pressing; it is a team out of position with a lot of company.

The press works because each player attacks a specific pass, not the ball carrier in general. In this setup:

- The **centre forward** presses the ball-side centre-back. This is the initial trigger — everything else waits for it.
- The **inside forward on the ball side** presses their full-back. If the centre-back's only safe pass is to the full-back, that is exactly where the trap closes.
- The **far-side inside forward** tucks inside to screen the switch of play. If he stays wide, the opponent's escape route is always available.
- The **box-to-box midfielder** steps onto their deepest midfielder. This is the second line of the press, and it only joins once the ball has gone backwards or sideways.
- The **deep-lying playmaker** never joins. He stays in front of the back four and screens. He is the reason a failed press is not a goal.

If you take one instruction list from this article, take those five roles and the fact that only two of them press aggressively at any moment.

The asymmetry is deliberate. The right-back attacks and provides width in the final third; the left-back stays on Support and is part of the rest defence. Two attacking full-backs in a pressing system is how you concede four on the counter — the press commits eight players forward, and if both full-backs are among them, the two centre-backs are defending alone.

In midfield, one runner and one sitter. The Channel Midfielder on Attack is the one arriving in the box as the striker pulls wide. The Deep-Lying Playmaker on Defend is the one who never leaves the space in front of the centre-backs. A midfield three of three runners is not a gegenpress; it is a team that will be counter-attacked to death.

## Instructions, by phase

**In possession:** Fairly Wide width, Shorter passing, Higher tempo, Play Out of Defence. The width matters because the full-back overlap is the main crossing source, and a narrow pitch removes it.

**In transition:** Counter on, **Counter-Press on** — this is the six-second rule, and it is the whole system — and Roll It Out for the keeper. The restart is short because a long kick out of a pressing shape is a turnover waiting to happen.

**Out of possession:** Line of Engagement Much Higher, Defensive Line Higher, Trigger Press Much More Often, Prevent Short GK Distribution on, Offside Trap on. Note the mismatch that confuses people: engagement Much Higher, defensive line only Higher. An engagement line at the halfway point with a defensive line pushed any further is how a fast striker gets three one-on-ones.

## What happens after the six seconds

This is the part almost every gegenpress guide skips, and it is where matches are actually won.

If the ball is not recovered within roughly six seconds, the press is off. What the team does next determines whether the tactic is viable over a season. The correct behaviour is a drop into a compact 4-5-1: both inside forwards fall to the flanks of midfield, the channel midfielder joins the central pair, and the shape becomes five across the middle in front of a back four.

In FM26 you cannot script this transition directly, but you can make it happen:

- Keep Trigger Press on Much More Often rather than "always closing down" individual instructions. Team-level pressing instructions drop back into shape; individual ones do not.
- Do not give the inside forwards "Close Down Much More" as an individual instruction. Let the team instruction handle it, or they will chase into full-back positions and never be in the five.
- The moment you see the press failing consistently — usually late in the first half — drop the engagement line one notch manually. It is not a defeat to play a 4-5-1 for twenty minutes.

## Fatigue is the real limiter

A gegenpress spends more condition than any other system in FM26, and the consequences are not subtle. Around the hour mark, your box-to-box midfielder's pressing triggers stop firing, the second line of the press disappears, and the opponent plays straight through the middle of your shape.

Treat rotation as part of the tactic:

- **Stamina 16+ for the midfield two and the attacking full-back.** A BBM on 12 Stamina is a liability, not a squad option.
- **Two players per pressing position.** If you have one box-to-box midfielder and no deputy, your system has a scheduled failure on the 65-minute mark.
- **Substitutions by minute 60, not by scoreline.** This is the one system where the substitution pattern is determined before kick-off.

## When the opponent refuses to play out

Against a side that defends in a 5-4-1 and kicks it long, there is no build-up to press. The trigger never fires, your front three press a keeper who is going to punt it anyway, and the block sits 60 metres away from where your players are standing.

Do not raise the press further. Change what the press is for. The gegenpress becomes a possession system, and the shape changes with it:

Alongside the shape change: Line of Engagement down to **Higher** (or Standard against a very deep block), Trigger Press down to **More Often**, and keep Counter-Press on for the moments you do win it high. The attacking midfielder exists to receive between the lines, which is the one thing a passive opponent cannot defend by standing still.

Accept the consequence: you will dominate the ball and create fewer clear chances than usual. Games like this are won by a set piece or a moment, not by the system.

## Reading the press in the match viewer

Pressing is easy to watch and hard to read, because intensity looks like success. A team chasing the ball all over the pitch produces an impressive highlight reel and loses 2-1 to two simple passes. The signals that tell you whether the press is actually working are quieter than that.

**Count the players behind the ball, not the ones pressing it.** Five seconds after a turnover, you should see five or more players goal-side of the ball. If it is four, the shape is not recovering and you will be countered eventually.

**Watch the opponent's first pass after winning possession.** A working press forces a rushed, backwards or sideways pass. A failing press lets their defender pick his head up and play it forward into midfield, which usually means your second line has stopped stepping.

**Watch the far-side winger.** He should be tucked in, screening the switch, whenever the ball is on the opposite flank. If he is standing on the touchline waiting for an attack, the opponent's easiest escape route is permanently open, and that is a positional instruction you can fix in ten seconds.

**Watch your box-to-box midfielder's condition at half time.** If he is below 70% at the break, the second line of the press will not survive the last thirty minutes regardless of what the instructions say. That is a substitution decision, not a tactical one.

You can watch all four of those without pausing the match if you know what you are looking for. The one that matters most is the first: a press that recovers one player short of a defensive shape is not a press, it is a decision to defend with fewer people.

## Game state: when to turn it off

A pressing system has a game state where it stops being an advantage, and recognising it early is worth more than any instruction tweak.

**When you are 2-0 up with 25 minutes left**, the gegenpress is working against you. The opponent is now going to play more directly, your midfielders are tired, and the trigger map will fail exactly when the game is most open. Drop the line of engagement to Higher, drop Trigger Press to Standard, and keep Counter-Press on for the moments it happens naturally. You are not abandoning the system; you are using the version of it that protects a lead.

**When the opponent brings on a second striker**, the press becomes harder to trigger and easier to bypass. Their extra forward means one more passing outlet past your second line. Consider dropping to the low-block variant for the last twenty minutes rather than chasing a press that now has an extra receiver to cover.

**When you are chasing a game**, resist the urge to raise everything. Much More Often pressing plus a Much Higher line when you are already losing is how a 1-0 defeat becomes a 3-0 defeat. Raise the tempo and push the attacking full-back higher, but keep the rest defence intact — you will get more chances from an opponent pinned in their own half than from a broken shape.

The mentality slider deserves a mention here too. Going Attacking or Overload to chase a game does not just add urgency; it changes player positioning across the whole shape and effectively overrides parts of the press structure you spent the match building. If you want to press harder, change the pressing instructions. Do not reach for the mentality setting.

## What this tactic needs

Physically demanding and technically lighter than the possession systems, which is why it is a good fit for mid-table and upper-mid-table squads:

1. **Work Rate and Stamina 16+ for the midfield pair and the attacking full-back.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Acceleration 15+ for the front three.** Pressing is about the first two steps, not top speed.
3. **Off the Ball 14+ for the forwards.** The trigger map depends on them reading the pass.
4. **Passing 14+ for the pivot and the ball-playing centre-back.** The first pass after a turnover decides whether the counter is on.
5. **Bravery and Aggression 14+ across the middle.** This is a contact system.

## When to switch to something else

**Your squad is technical but not athletic.** Then the press will work for one half and fail for the other. The possession systems solve this differently — the [Guardiola 3-2-5](/blog/guardiola-tactics-fm26) and the [De Zerbi build-up](/blog/de-zerbi-tactics-fm26) both ask for calm rather than stamina, and the [five control systems compared](/blog/fm26-positional-control-managers) sorts them by how much defending they demand.

**You want to press, but your centre-backs are slow.** A high line plus a slow defence is the fastest way to lose. The lane-based press in that same comparison article can be dialled down when the press fails, which this one cannot.

**You are getting beaten by exactly this system and want to know how to survive it.** The [low block and counter systems compared](/blog/fm26-low-block-counter-managers) covers the three structures best equipped to sit through 90 minutes of it.

## Installing it

1. Set the 4-3-3 roles from the board, then the three instruction blocks. Do not add individual pressing instructions to the forwards — the team instructions are the system.
2. Check that your attacking full-back has a defender behind him. If both full-backs are on Attack, fix that before you play a match.
3. Play one friendly and watch the first ten seconds after every turnover. If five players chase the ball, the trigger map has not been understood. If two press and three drop, it has.
4. Set your substitution plan before kick-off. Two midfielders and the attacking full-back off around the hour mark.
5. Judge it over a season of squad rotation, not a month of your best eleven. This tactic does not survive being played by tired players, and a squad that is not built for it will make the system look worse than it is.
