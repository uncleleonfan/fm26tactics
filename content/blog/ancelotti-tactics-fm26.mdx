## Ancelotti Tactics in FM26: The Philosophy

Carlo Ancelotti doesn't build the most complex systems — he builds the most **efficient** ones. Real Madrid's game plan is simple in theory and brutal in execution: stay compact, don't chase the ball, and let a front line of superstars win the game in the final third.

The core principles:

1. **The mid-block** — defend between the halfway line and your own box, never stretch the shape
2. **Low-risk build-up** — short passes through the double pivot, no suicide balls
3. **Vertical transitions** — the moment the ball is won, the front three attack with pace
4. **Superstar freedom** — give the elite players licence, not instructions

## The Ancelotti Formation in FM26

```
              SK (Su)

FB(S)   CD(D)   CD(D)   FB(S)

       DM(D)   DLP(S)

IW(S)          AM(A)
       PF(A)
       AF(A)
```

**This is a 4-2-3-1 that defends as a 4-4-2.** The AM(A) drops onto the pivot when the opponent has the ball, turning the front three into a two-man line with the striker. In possession, the full-backs push wide and the shape opens into a 4-3-3.

| Position | Role | Ancelotti Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Support)** | Comfortable receiving, quick to release |
| CDs | **CD(D) + CD(D)** | No ball-playing risks — defend the box, head everything |
| Full-backs | **FB(S) + FB(S)** | Wide in possession, tucked when defending — balance first |
| Midfield | **DM(D) + DLP(S)** | The pivot screens the counter and launches the vertical pass |
| Attacking Mid | **AM(A)** | The Bellingham role — arrives late, wins the box, presses the pivot |
| Wingers | **IW(S) + IW(S)** | Stay wide to isolate full-backs in 1v1s |
| Strikers | **PF(A) + AF(A)** | The front two press, then attack the space in behind |

## Team Instructions: The Ancelotti Settings

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Fairly Wide | Isolate the wide forwards 1v1 against full-backs |
| Passing | Shorter | Low-risk progression through the pivot |
| Tempo | Standard | Patient until the transition moment arrives |
| Play Out of Defence | ON | Build through the pivot, avoid long clearances |
| Work Ball Into Box | OFF | Allow the front three freedom — they earn shots |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ON | The entire system is built for the vertical moment |
| Counter-Press | OFF | Recover shape first — Real Madrid don't chase the ball |
| GK Distribution | Distribute Quickly | Release before the opponent resets |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Line of Engagement | **Standard** | The mid-block — invite the opponent to play in front of you |
| Defensive Line | Standard | Compact between the lines, protect the space in behind |
| Trigger Press | Default | Press only when the opponent is in the middle third |
| Prevent Short GK Dist | OFF | Allow short build-up — the block is the weapon |
| Defensive Width | Narrow | Force play into the crowded middle, win it, break |

## The Art of Superstar Freedom

Ancelotti's greatest tactical trick is knowing **when not to instruct**. In FM26 terms:

1. **Don't mark tightly** — remove tight marking on your best attacker so they drift into dangerous pockets
2. **Allow roaming** — let the AM and wide forwards Roam From Position; their decisions beat any preset
3. **Keep individual instructions minimal** — add one PPM-style instruction per star, never a checklist
4. **Set high Free Role trust** — a system that over-coaches Vini or Bellingham turns them into average players

The balance: rigid structure out of possession, complete freedom in the final third. This is why Ancelotti has won the Champions League five times.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Pace & Acceleration 15+ for the front three** — the transition lives and dies on the first sprint
2. **Work Rate 15+ for the pivot** — they cover the counter both ways
3. **Heading & Jumping Reach 15+ for the CBs** — the block concedes crosses; win them all
4. **Composure 14+ for the DLP** — the vertical pass must be accurate under pressure
5. **Finishing 15+ for the strikers** — this system creates few chances, and they must be buried

## Common Mistakes

### Pressing too high
The mid-block is the system. If you push the line of engagement to Much Higher, you abandon the compact shape that makes the transition work — and you'll be exposed in behind by faster teams.

### Counter-press on
Real Madrid *don't* counter-press. Turning it on burns your superstars' stamina and drags the block out of shape. Keep Counter-Press OFF and recover the 4-4-2 first.

### Over-coaching the stars
Adding five individual instructions to a world-class winger removes their instincts. In FM26, elite players with high Flair and Off the Ball perform best with minimal direction.

## When Ancelotti's System Struggles

- **Against possession monsters** (Man City, Barcelona) — the mid-block concedes 60%+ possession and suffers in sustained attacks. In these games, drop the defensive line slightly and rely on the transition.
- **Against teams that also sit deep** — if the opponent never attacks, there's no space to break into. Add "Be More Expressive" and let your stars create from nothing.
