## Nagelsmann Tactics in FM26: The Philosophy

Julian Nagelsmann is the youngest tactical radical in modern football: a coach who treats the defensive line as an **attacking weapon**. His teams press vertically, pin the opponent in their own half, and turn the halfway line into a war of attrition.

The core principles:

1. **The extreme high line** — defend at the halfway line, not your own box
2. **Vertical pressing** — cut the passing lanes ahead of the ball, don't chase it sideways
3. **Free-roaming attackers** — the front four swap positions constantly
4. **Patient build-up, sudden acceleration** — control the rhythm, then strike

## The Nagelsmann Formation in FM26

```
              SK (Su)

FB(A)  BPD(D)  CD(D)   FB(A)

       DM(D)   BBM(S)

IW(S)          SS(A)
       AM(A)
       PF(A)
```

**The 4-2-3-1 is the base, but the front four are never where they start.** The wide forwards tuck in, the number 10 drops into midfield, and the striker drifts wide — the opponent can never mark a fixed shape.

| Position | Role | Nagelsmann Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Support)** | Plays 20 metres off his line — the last line of the press |
| CDs | **BPD(D) + CD(D)** | Fast, comfortable on the ball, quick to step up |
| Full-backs | **FB(A) ×2** | Provide the width while the wide forwards tuck in |
| Midfield | **DM(D) + BBM(S)** | The DM screens; the BBM presses and arrives late |
| Attacking Mid | **AM(A)** | The link — drops to receive, then attacks the box |
| Wingers | **IW(S) + SS(A)** | Tuck inside to overload the half-spaces |
| Striker | **Pressing Forward (Attack)** | Leads the press and stretches the line |

## Vertical Pressing: Pressing the Lanes, Not the Ball

Nagelsmann's press is different from Klopp's. Klopp chases the ball; Nagelsmann **cuts the lanes**:

1. **The front four press in a diamond** — they block the passing lanes into midfield, not the man
2. **The DM steps up** — the second line of the press joins the front line on trigger
3. **The full-backs push high** — the opponent's wide players are pinned in their own half
4. **The keeper is the sweeper** — the space behind the high line is his responsibility

In FM26 this means setting the line of engagement and defensive line to Much Higher, then relying on the keeper's Sweeper Keeper (Support) duty to cover the space behind.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Pace 15+ for the back four** — recovery speed when the trap fails
2. **Passing & First Touch 14+** — the vertical game demands it
3. **Positioning & Decisions 15+ for defenders** — the line lives and dies here
4. **Stamina 16+ for full-backs and the BBM** — width and pressing both ways
5. **Acceleration 15+ for the front four** — pressing triggers rely on explosive first steps

## Common Mistakes

### The line breaks
One defender stepping up late ruins the whole trap. Build full tactical familiarity before deploying the high line, and check your CBs' Decisions and Positioning every match.

### Static front four
The rotation is the system. If the wide forwards hug the touchline and the AM stands still, the pressing diamond and the half-space overloads both disappear. Use Be More Expressive and Roam From Position.

### A traditional keeper
A stay-on-line goalkeeper with the high line is a disaster — every through ball becomes a 1v1. The Sweeper Keeper role is mandatory in this system.

---

## When Nagelsmann's System Struggles

- **Against pacey direct strikers** — a Much Higher line is suicide against rapid front men. Drop to Higher and turn the offside trap off in these games.
- **Against deep blocks** — if the opponent never plays out, the vertical press has nothing to press. Drop the line of engagement to Higher and let your full-backs become crossing threats.
