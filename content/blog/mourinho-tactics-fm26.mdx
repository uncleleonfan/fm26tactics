## Mourinho Tactics in FM26: The Philosophy

José Mourinho built a trophy cabinet on a simple, ruthless idea: **the team that concedes nothing, wins**. His systems don't chase the ball — they let the opponent have it where it's harmless, then strike with two or three devastating passes.

The core principles:

1. **The low block** — defend in the final third of the pitch, compact and narrow
2. **Two banks of five** — a 5-4-1 out of possession that leaves no space anywhere
3. **Let them have it** — possession in harmless areas is the opponent's problem
4. **Two-pass transitions** — win the ball, find the front two, done

---

## The Mourinho Formation in FM26

```
              SK (D)

CD(D)   CD(D)   CD(D)

WB(S)              WB(S)
       DM(D)   BBM(S)
       BBM(S)
        DLF(S)
        AF(A)
```

**In defence this is a 5-4-1.** The wing-backs drop to form a back five, the two BBM(s) sit narrow as a midfield four, and the AF chases alone until the transition. In attack the wing-backs release and the front two combine.

| Position | Role | Mourinho Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Defend)** | Conservative — a shot-stopper first, distribution second |
| Back three | **CD(D) ×3** | No ball-playing risks — clear the danger, win the headers |
| Wing-backs | **WB(S) ×2** | Defend as full-backs, attack only on the transition |
| Midfield | **DM(D) + 2× BBM(S)** | The DM screens; the BBMs press in pairs and hold the second ball |
| Strikers | **DLF(S) + AF(A)** | The link and the runner — two passes from box to box |

## Team Instructions: The Mourinho Settings

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Fairly Narrow | Compact in the middle — protect the counter first |
| Passing | More Direct | Bypass the press, find the front two early |
| Tempo | Higher | The transition must happen before the opponent resets |
| Play Out of Defence | OFF | Clear the danger — don't invite pressure in your own third |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ON | The entire attacking plan — win it, release the front two |
| Counter-Press | **OFF** | Never chase the ball. Recover the shape, always |
| GK Distribution | Distribute Quickly | Long and early to the DLF or the flanks |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Line of Engagement | **Standard/Lower** | The low block — defend between the box and the halfway line |
| Defensive Line | Standard | Compact with the midfield — no space in behind |
| Trigger Press | Less Often | Press only when the opponent enters the block |
| Prevent Short GK Dist | OFF | Allow the opponent possession — it's harmless |
| Defensive Width | Narrow | Pack the middle — force the play wide, then defend the cross |
| Tackling | Stay on Feet | Discipline over aggression — no free kicks near the box |

## Game Management: Mourinho's Real Superpower

Mourinho's greatest strength isn't the formation — it's **game state**. In FM26 this translates to match-day management:

1. **First 60 minutes: the block** — let the opponent dominate the ball, stay compact
2. **First goal: drop even deeper** — go to a Lower line of engagement, time-waste, kill the game
3. **At 1-0 with 20 minutes left: the 5-4-1** — swap the DLF for a third CB or a defensive midfielder
4. **Against top sides: target set pieces** — Mourinho teams win games from corners and free-kicks

The block isn't passive — it's a strategy. Every minute the opponent spends passing sideways in your third is a minute of frustration that breeds the error you'll punish.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Strength 15+ & Jumping Reach 15+ for the back three** — you concede crosses; win them all
2. **Work Rate 16+ for the midfield** — they close lanes for 90 minutes
3. **Pace 15+ for the AF** — the entire attack is a two-pass race
4. **Heading 15+ for the DLF** — the out-ball must stick when the block clears
5. **Composure 14+ under pressure** — the block must not panic in the final 20 minutes

## Common Mistakes

### Pressing the press
The moment you turn the trigger press up, the block stretches and the shape dies. Mourinho's teams are *patient* — Less Often is a setting, not an insult.

### Both wing-backs attacking
Two attacking wing-backs in a 3-5-2 leave the back three exposed on both flanks. The transition attacks through the *front two* — the wing-backs support, they don't lead.

### Forgetting the set-piece plan
Mourinho's 1-0 wins come from corners and free-kicks more than open play. Assign dedicated routines, target the tallest CBs, and make set pieces a core part of the plan — not an afterthought.

## When Mourinho's System Struggles

- **Against teams that also sit deep** — two low blocks make for a boring 0-0. In these games, raise the line of engagement to Standard and trust the front two's quality.
- **Against elite crossing sides** — the block concedes 20+ crosses a game. If the opponent has a dominant target man, add "Mark Tightly" on their striker and the tallest midfielder onto him.
