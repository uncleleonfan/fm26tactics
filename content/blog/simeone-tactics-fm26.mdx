## Simeone Tactics in FM26: The Philosophy

Diego Simeone built Atlético Madrid's golden era on **competitive rage**: a 4-4-2 that defends like a wall, fights for every ball, and punishes the opponent in two passes. It isn't pretty — it's effective, and it has won La Liga, two Europa Leagues, and reached two Champions League finals.

The core principles:

1. **Two banks of four** — the most compact 4-4-2 in football, no space between the lines
2. **Man-marking discipline** — the key threat is always covered
3. **Aggression as strategy** — tactical fouls, hard tackles, and mental pressure
4. **Direct transitions** — win it, find the front two, done

---

## The Simeone Formation in FM26

```
              SK (D)

FB(D)   CD(D)   CD(D)   FB(D)

WM(S)  BBM(D)  DLP(S)  WM(S)

       DLF(S)
       AF(A)
```

**The 4-4-2 is the base, but the shape is never static.** The wide midfielders tuck in to form a midfield four that sits 10-15 metres from the back line, while the front two drop to protect the middle third.

| Position | Role | Simeone Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Defend)** | A shot-stopper first — never a playmaker |
| CDs | **CD(D) ×2** | Physical, aggressive, dominant in the air |
| Full-backs | **FB(D) ×2** | Defend first, support the transition occasionally |
| Midfield | **BBM(D) + DLP(S)** | The destroyer screens; the DLP holds the second ball |
| Wide Midfield | **WM(S) ×2** | Track back, tuck in, support the counter |
| Strikers | **DLF(S) + AF(A)** | The link and the runner — the entire attack |

## Team Instructions: The Simeone Settings

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Narrow | Compact in the middle — protect the counter first |
| Passing | More Direct | Bypass the press, find the front two early |
| Tempo | Higher | The transition must happen before the reset |
| Play Out of Defence | OFF | Clear the danger — never invite pressure |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ON | The entire attacking plan — win it, release the front two |
| Counter-Press | OFF | Never chase the ball. Recover the shape, always |
| GK Distribution | Distribute Quickly | Long and early to the DLF or the flanks |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Line of Engagement | Standard/Lower | The block — defend in your own half |
| Defensive Line | Standard | Compact with the midfield — no space in behind |
| Trigger Press | Less Often | Press in coordinated bursts, not constantly |
| Defensive Width | Narrow | Pack the middle — force play wide, defend the cross |
| Tackling | **Harder** | The Simeone signature — aggression wins the first ball |
| Mark Tightly | **ON** | Man-mark the key threats, never give them a free touch |

## Aggression as a System

Simeone's teams don't just defend — they **intimidate**. In FM26 this translates to:

1. **Tackling Harder + Mark Tightly** — the opponent's best player is never alone on the ball
2. **Team mentality: Balanced → Positive only when behind** — never chase the game early
3. **Use Opposition Instructions aggressively** — close down the playmaker, show the striker onto his weak foot
4. **The tactical foul** — a midfield destroyer with high Aggression and Bravery disrupts rhythm without conceding a system break

The block alone doesn't win titles. The block + controlled aggression is what makes opponents panic in the final 20 minutes.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Aggression 16+ & Bravery 15+** — the entire style is built on the first challenge
2. **Work Rate 16+ for the midfield four** — they close lanes for 90 minutes
3. **Strength 15+ & Jumping Reach 15+ for the back four** — you concede crosses; win them all
4. **Pace 15+ for the AF** — the attack is a two-pass race
5. **Composure 14+ under pressure** — the block must not panic in the final 20 minutes

## Common Mistakes

### Both strikers pressing high
The front two must *not* chase the ball deep into the opponent's half. They hold the middle third, force play wide, and press only on trigger. Two strikers pressing high leaves a yawning gap behind them.

### Full-backs over-committing
Simeone's full-backs attack *rarely* — one at a time, and only when the transition is clearly on. Two overlapping full-backs in a 4-4-2 is how mid-table teams get picked apart.

### Forgetting the set-piece plan
Atlético's goals come from corners and free-kicks as often as open play. Assign dedicated routines, target the tallest CBs, and make set pieces a core part of the plan.

---

## When Simeone's System Struggles

- **Against elite possession sides** — the block can hold for 70 minutes, but sustained pressure finds errors. In these games, add "Stay On Feet" to avoid giving away free kicks around the box.
- **Against teams that also sit deep** — two low blocks make for a frustrating 0-0. Raise the line of engagement to Standard and trust the front two's quality in the final third.
