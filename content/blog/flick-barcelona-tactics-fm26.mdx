## Flick Barcelona in FM26: The Philosophy

Hansi Flick turned Barcelona's crisis into a 2025 La Liga title with a simple, radical idea: **push the line so high that the offside trap becomes a defensive weapon**. Instead of defending their own box, Barcelona defend the halfway line.

The core principles:

1. **The line as the defender** — play the opponent offside before they reach the box
2. **Vertical football** — quick, forward passes; no sterile possession
3. **Width from the full-backs** — the flanks attack while wingers cut inside
4. **One-touch press** — win it high, then go direct

## The Flick Formation in FM26

```
              SK (Su)

FB(A)   BPD(D)   CD(D)   FB(A)

       DM(D)   DLP(S)

IW(S)          IF(A)
       AM(A)

            PF(A)

```

| Position | Role | Flick Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Support)** | Acts as a sweeper behind the halfway-line defensive line |
| CDs | **BPD(D) + CD(D)** | Comfortable on the ball, quick to step up for the trap |
| Full-backs | **Full-Back (Attack) ×2** | Provide width — the wingers come inside |
| Midfield | **DM(D) + DLP(S)** | The pivot screens; the DLP launches vertical passes |
| Attacking Mid | **Advanced Playmaker (Attack)** | The link between midfield and the front line |
| Wingers | **IW(S) + IF(A)** | Cut inside, press the full-backs, attack the box |
| Striker | **Pressing Forward (Attack)** | Leads the press and the line |

## Team Instructions: The Flick Settings

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Fairly Wide | Full-backs stretch the pitch |
| Passing | Shorter | Controlled build-up, quick combinations |
| Tempo | Higher | Vertical, not tiki-taka patience |
| Play Out of Defence | ON | Build through the press |
| Work Ball Into Box | ON | Create quality chances, not long shots |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ON | Win it high, go forward immediately |
| Counter-Press | ON | The 6-second rule lives on here too |
| GK Distribution | Roll It Out | Short restart of the build-up |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Line of Engagement | Much Higher | Press in the opposition half |
| Defensive Line | **Much Higher** | The defining setting — defend the halfway line |
| Trigger Press | Much More Often | Constant pressure on the ball |
| Prevent Short GK Dist | ON | Force long kicks |
| **Offside Trap** | **ON** | The trap is the defensive system |
| Defensive Width | Force Inside | Push play wide, then trap them |

## The Offside Trap as a Skill

The trap only works if the back line is perfectly coordinated. Before deploying Flick's system:

1. **Check your CBs' Decisions and Positioning (15+ recommended)** — one misstep is a 1v1
2. **Train the line** — the trap needs full tactical familiarity
3. **Wing-backs and the pivot must hold the same line** — a broken line is fatal

Against teams with pacey strikers who time runs early, turn the trap OFF and drop to a Standard line. The trap is a weapon, not a religion.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Acceleration 15+ for CBs** — recovery pace when the trap fails
2. **Passing & First Touch 14+** — the vertical game demands it
3. **Positioning & Decisions 15+ for defenders** — the trap lives and dies here
4. **Stamina 16+ for full-backs** — width both ways
5. **Composure 14+ under pressure** — Barcelona build from the back against the press

## Common Mistakes

### Trap without a coordinated line
One defender stepping up late ruins the whole system. Use the training ground to build familiarity before deploying it in competitive matches.

### Slow center-backs
The high line needs recovery pace. Two slow CBs with an offside trap is a recipe for 1v1s every game.

### Possession without purpose
Flick's Barcelona is NOT tiki-taka. If your players pass sideways for minutes, raise the tempo and add "Pass Into Space."
