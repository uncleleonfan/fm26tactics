## Alonso Tactics in FM26: The Philosophy

Xabi Alonso built Bayer Leverkusen's invincible season on a system that **blends everything**: the positional control of Guardiola, the vertical transition of Ancelotti, and the counter-press of Klopp. The 3-4-2-1 is the shape; the flexibility is the philosophy.

The core principles:

1. **The double 10** — two free-roaming attacking midfielders who never sit still
2. **Wing-back width** — the flanks are the wing-backs' lane, always
3. **Blend possession with transition** — control the game, but strike the moment it opens
4. **The counter-press as a foundation** — win it back high, immediately

## The Alonso Formation in FM26

```
              SK (Su)

BPD(D)  CD(D)  BPD(D)

WB(A)              WB(S)
       DM(D)   BBM(S)

       SS(A)   SS(A)
        DLF(A)
```

**This is the shape that went unbeaten.** The back three build with a spare man against any press, the wing-backs provide constant width, and the double 10s + DLF create a front three that rotates relentlessly.

| Position | Role | Alonso Logic |
|---|---|---|
| GK | **Sweeper Keeper (Support)** | Comfortable sweeping behind the high line |
| Back three | **BPD(D) + CD(D) + BPD(D)** | Three at the back — always one spare man in build-up |
| Wing-backs | **WB(A) + WB(S)** | One attacks the box, one holds width — never symmetrical |
| Midfield | **DM(D) + BBM(S)** | The DM screens the counter; the BBM presses and arrives late |
| Double 10 | **SS(A) + SS(A)** | The Wirtz role ×2 — roam the half-spaces, never stand still |
| Striker | **Deep-Lying Forward (Attack)** | Drops to link the midfield, then attacks the box |

## Team Instructions: The Alonso Settings

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Fairly Wide | The wing-backs need the pitch stretched to attack 1v1 |
| Passing | Mixed | Patient build-up, but direct when the transition opens |
| Tempo | Higher | Leverkusen don't hold the ball for its own sake |
| Play Out of Defence | ON | The back three beats the first press with the spare man |
| Pass Into Space | ON | Release the double 10s and DLF in behind |
| Overlap | Both Sides | The wing-backs bomb on constantly |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Counter | ON | The vertical moment is a core weapon, not an option |
| Counter-Press | ON | Win it back in 5 seconds — the invincible season was built here |
| GK Distribution | Roll It Out | Short restart into the back three |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Line of Engagement | Higher | Press in the middle third — the double 10s join the DLF |
| Defensive Line | Higher | Squeeze with the midfield; the back three covers the line |
| Trigger Press | More Often | Trap triggers on passes into the central channels |
| Prevent Short GK Dist | ON | Force long kicks, win the second ball with the back three |
| Defensive Width | Narrow | Compact in the centre — funnel play wide to the wing-backs |

## The Double 10: Leverkusen's Secret Weapon

The two shadow strikers are what separates this system from a standard 3-4-2-1. They are **not** wingers and **not** strikers — they're free-roaming playmakers in the final third:

1. **Never stand still** — set both to Roam From Position and high individual pressing
2. **Swap constantly** — one drops, one attacks; the centre-backs can never mark both
3. **They press as the front three** — the SS + DLF form the first line of the counter-press
4. **Attribute priorities** — Off the Ball 15+, First Touch 15+, Flair 13+, Finishing 13+

In FM26 this is the Wirtz role multiplied by two. If your double 10s stand still, the system becomes a lifeless 3-5-2.

## Key Player Requirements

1. **Stamina 16+ for the wing-backs** — they cover the entire flank, both ways, all game
2. **Off the Ball 15+ for the double 10s** — the rotation is the entire attack
3. **Pace 14+ for the back three** — the high line needs recovery speed
4. **Passing 15+ for the BPDs and DM** — the vertical pass must be one-touch accurate
5. **Work Rate 16+ for the midfield pair** — they press, screen, and arrive late

## Common Mistakes

### Symmetrical wing-backs
Two attacking wing-backs leave the back three exposed on both flanks. Alonso always keeps one balanced — one WB(A), one WB(S), and they swap sides by match plan.

### Treating the 10s as wingers
If the SSs drift wide, they clog the wing-backs' lane and leave the middle empty. Their lane is the half-space — never the touchline.

### Static build-up
The 3-4-2-1 can drift into sterile possession. If your team passes sideways for minutes, the tempo is too low — raise it and add "Pass Into Space" until the transitions return.

---

## When Alonso's System Struggles

- **Against elite wide attackers** — the wing-backs' lane is the vulnerability. Against rapid wingers, drop both to Support and add "Tackle Harder" on the wide players.
- **Against deep 5-at-the-back blocks** — the double 10s find no space in behind. Add "Work Ball Into Box" and rely on the wing-backs' crossing into the DLF.
