# 4-4-1-1 Counter-Attack: The Complete FM26 Guide

The 4-4-1-1 is Jose Mourinho's signature formation — a deep block with two banks of four, a shadow striker playing off a target forward, and devastating counter-attacks. In Football Manager 2026, it's the perfect system for underdog sides and teams that want to absorb pressure and strike on the break.

## Part 1: Tactical Philosophy

### What the 4-4-1-1 Counter-Attack Does in FM26

- **Phase 1 — The Wall** (out of possession): Two flat banks of four — four defenders and four midfielders — form an impenetrable block. The shadow striker (Trequartista) sits between the lines, ready to launch the counter. The opposition is forced into patient possession around the block with no space to penetrate.

- **Phase 2 — The Break** (transition): The moment possession is won, the ball goes to the Trequartista or the DLP. The wingers sprint forward to provide width, the TF holds off the CB, and the counter is launched in 3-5 seconds. The opposition's attacking shape is disorganised — perfect for exploitation.

- **Phase 3 — The Strike** (attacking): The Trequartista finds the wingers or the TF, and the attack is finished quickly. No prolonged possession — the goal is to score within 5-7 seconds of winning the ball.

### Formation Shape

```
                    TF(A)
               T(A)

   W(A)    BWM(D)    DLP(D)    W(A)

   FB(D)    CD(D)    CD(D)    FB(D)

                    GK(D)
```

> **Key**: The Trequartista is the "shadow striker" — he drops into the hole between the opposition midfield and defence, linking the defensive block to the TF.

## Part 2: Team Instructions

### In Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Attacking Width** | Fairly Wide | Wingers provide width. The T and TF operate centrally. |
| **Passing Directness** | Much More Direct | Counters require vertical urgency. |
| **Tempo** | Higher | Speed catches defences unset. |
| **Play Out of Defence** | OFF | Go direct on the counter. No slow build-up. |
| **Pass Into Space** | ON | The wingers and TF make runs into space. |
| **Hit Early Crosses** | ON | Wingers should cross before the defence recovers. |
| **Work Ball Into Box** | OFF | Direct shots are encouraged. |

### In Transition

| Instruction | Setting | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Counter** | ON | The T and wingers sprint forward immediately. |
| **Counter-Press** | OFF | Retreat into the 4-4-1-1 block. |
| **GK Distribution** | Long Kick to TF | Skip the build-up phase. |
| **Distribute Quickly** | ON | Release before the opposition resets. |

### Out of Possession

| Instruction | Setting | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Defensive Line** | Much Lower | Deep block — compress space in the defensive third. |
| **Line of Engagement** | Low Block | Start pressing in the defensive third. |
| **Trigger Press** | Less Often | Hold position — let the opposition come into the block. |
| **Defensive Width** | Force Outside | Two banks of four cover the centre. Inviting crosses is fine. |
| **Get Stuck In** | ON | Physical battles in deep areas win the ball back. |
| **Offside Trap** | OFF | Deep line = offside trap is too risky. |

## Part 3: Player Roles

### Goalkeeper: Goalkeeper (Defend)

```
Must-have: Reflexes 14+, Aerial Reach 14+, Handling 13+, Kicking 13+
PPMs: "Uses Long Throws to Start Counter Attacks"
```

Traditional shot-stopper. The deep block means no sweeping required. Good Kicking to start counters with long throws to the wingers or TF.

### Centre-Backs: CD (Defend) × 2

```
Must-have: Marking 15+, Tackling 14+, Positioning 15+, Jumping Reach 14+
PPMs: "Marks Opponent Tightly", "Stays Back at All Times"
```

Two disciplined defenders. They hold the line, win aerial duels, and clear long. No roaming, no carrying the ball — just defend.

### Full-Backs: FB (Defend) × 2

```
Must-have: Marking 14+, Tackling 13+, Positioning 14+, Stamina 14+
PPMs: "Stays Back at All Times"
```

The FBs stay deep to form the back four. They don't overlap — that's the wingers' job. Their primary role is to defend and provide a safe passing option for the counter-launch.

### Midfield: The Flat Four

#### Winger (Attack) × 2

```
Must-have: Pace 15+, Crossing 14+, Dribbling 13+, Stamina 14+
PPMs: "Runs with Ball Down Left/Right", "Hits Early Crosses"
```

The wingers are the primary counter-attacking outlets. When the ball is won, they sprint forward with the ball and cross early. On Attack duty to maximize the counter threat — they don't track back deep.

#### Ball-Winning Midfielder (Defend)

```
Must-have: Tackling 15+, Work Rate 15+, Stamina 15+, Aggression 14+
PPMs: "Stays Back at All Times", "Dives Into Tackles"
```

The destroyer. Sits in front of the back four, wins the ball, and lays off to the DLP. Never ventures forward.

#### Deep-Lying Playmaker (Defend)

```
Must-have: Passing 15+, Vision 15+, Composure 14+, Decisions 14+
PPMs: "Tries Killer Balls Often", "Switches Ball to Other Flank"
```

The counter-launcher. Receives from the BWM and hits long balls to the wingers or through balls to the T. The DLP is the brain of every counter-attack.

### The Shadow Striker: Trequartista (Attack)

```
Must-have: Technique 15+, Vision 14+, Dribbling 14+, Off The Ball 14+
PPMs: "Tries Killer Balls Often", "Runs with Ball Through Centre", "Comes Deep to Get Ball"
```

The key player. The Trequartista drops into the hole between midfield and attack, receives the ball from the DLP, and creates chances for the TF and wingers. He's the creative heartbeat of the counter-attack — every transition goes through him.

### The Lone Striker: Target Forward (Attack)

```
Must-have: Jumping Reach 15+, Strength 14+, Heading 14+, First Touch 13+
PPMs: "Plays With Back to Goal", "Knocks Ball Past Opponent"
```

The outlet. He receives long balls, holds off the CB, and lays off to the T or wingers. His aerial ability is crucial — he's the target for goal kicks and long passes from the DLP.

## Part 4: Set Piece Integration

### Defensive Corners

Both CBs and the BWM in the 6-yard box. The T marks the edge for counter-launch. The TF stays forward as a counter threat. FBs mark the wide areas.

### Attacking Corners

The TF attacks near post. One CB attacks far post. The DLP takes the corner — aim near post, in-swinging. One winger stays back for defensive cover.

## Part 5: Opposition Instructions

| Position | Tight Mark | Press | Tackle | Show Onto |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBs | Always | Always | Hard | Weaker Foot |
| Full Backs | Never | Never | — | Weaker Foot |
| Wingers | Never | Never | Hard | Outside |
| AMC | Always | Always | Hard | Weaker Foot |
| Strikers | Never | Never | Hard | Weaker Foot |

Press the CBs to force long balls. Mark the AMC to neutralize the opposition's creativity. The two banks of four handle everything else.

## Part 6: In-Match Adjustments

### Protecting a Lead at Minute 70

- Drop mentality to **Defensive**
- Drop wingers to **Support** duty (more defensive)
- Time Wasting: **Frequently**
- Replace the T with a fresh BWM (switch to 4-5-1)

### Chasing a Goal at Minute 75

- Push mentality to **Attacking**
- Raise Line of Engagement to **Mid Block**
- Push FBs to **Support** duty for more width
- Replace the BWM with a second TF (switch to 4-4-2)

## Part 7: Strengths & Weaknesses

### Strengths

| Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Defensive solidity** | Two banks of four + a T between the lines = 10 players behind the ball. |
| **Counter efficiency** | T → wingers → TF creates 3-man counters in 3-5 seconds. |
| **Underdog-friendly** | The deep block is perfect for weaker sides against stronger opposition. |
| **Shadow striker creativity** | The T in the hole creates chances that a flat 4-4-1 cannot. |

### Weaknesses

| Weakness | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **No possession** | Accept it. 30-35% possession is normal. |
| **Struggles vs low blocks** | When you're the favorite, the opposition sits deep and there's no space for counters. Switch to a 4-2-3-1 Gegenpress in a second tactic slot. |
| **T dependency** | If the T is marked out, the counter-attack loses its creative hub. Have a backup with good Technique and Vision. |
| **Winger stamina** | Wingers sprint forward for counters and back for defence. Sub at 60-65 minutes. |

## Part 9: FAQ

**Q: How is the 4-4-1-1 different from the 4-4-2?**
A: The 4-4-2 (see [4-4-2 Wing Play](/tactics/4-4-2-wing-play)) has two strikers side by side. The 4-4-1-1 has one striker (TF) and a shadow striker (T) in the hole — the T creates from deep, the TF finishes. More defensive solidity, less aerial presence in the box.

**Q: Why a Trequartista and not an AMC?**
A: A Trequartista roams freely and drops deep to receive — perfect for the counter-attack. An AMC stays in a fixed position, which is too predictable. The T's movement makes him harder to mark.

**Q: Is this the most defensive formation in the game?**
A: No. The 5-3-2 Route One (see [5-3-2 Route One](/tactics/5-3-2-route-one)) has five CBs. But the 4-4-1-1 is the most defensively solid formation with four at the back — the two banks of four are extremely hard to break down.
